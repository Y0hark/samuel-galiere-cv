Services

What I do

I help teams reduce the risk of choosing the wrong AI/Data use case, building a useless prototype, or creating a tool that cannot be adopted or maintained.

01

Operational AI/Data assessment

I help clarify where AI/Data can actually create value by looking at business pain points, available data, workflows, risks and feasibility.

02

Use case prioritization

I score use cases based on operational impact, data availability, technical complexity, adoption risk and integration constraints.

03

AI/Data prototyping

I build limited-scope prototypes to test value quickly before scaling complexity: assistants, extraction workflows, dashboards, automation or decision-support tools.

04

Software & data architecture

I design architectures that connect prototypes to existing systems, APIs, data flows, security constraints and maintainability requirements.

05

From POC to production

I help teams move beyond demos by defining success criteria, failure criteria, integration needs, monitoring, documentation and ownership.

Scope

Typical use cases

The best AI/Data use case is not always the most spectacular one. In operations, value often starts with repeated friction.

  • Reporting automation and operational dashboards
  • Document and information extraction from PDFs, emails or spreadsheets
  • Internal assistants with verifiable sources
  • Data quality and reconciliation workflows
  • Transport, tracking or operational exception prioritization
  • Decision-support prototypes for supply chain teams
  • Workflow automation around existing business tools
  • AI/Data roadmap scoping for logistics and industrial operations
Method

How I work

01

Start from the operational decision

Before discussing models or tools, I clarify the decision, workflow or recurring pain point we want to improve.

02

Check the available data

I look at what data already exists, where it lives, how reliable it is, and what risks it creates.

03

Scope the smallest useful prototype

The goal is not to build a large system immediately, but to test value on a limited, measurable scope.

04

Design for integration

A useful prototype should already consider APIs, users, permissions, workflows, monitoring and maintainability.

05

Decide objectively

A good prototype should help decide whether to stop, reframe, extend or industrialize the use case.

Typical formats: a short scoping engagement to clarify and prioritize use cases, or a prototype sprint on a single defined scope. Both end with a documented decision, not a demo.
